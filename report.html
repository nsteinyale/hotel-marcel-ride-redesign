Side Project — Hotel Marcel Car Service Redesign

Live, schedulable rides between Hotel Marcel and Yale SOM

AuthorN Stein Repogithub.com/nsteinyale/hotel-marcel-ride-redesign Portfoliogithub.com/nsteinyale/mgt409-portfolio Live appnsteinyale.github.io/hotel-marcel-ride-redesign

This redesign turns Hotel Marcel's one-way car request into a two-way ride: students book their SOM ride ahead of time, watch the car's live progress and arrival time, and message the driver. The free service stays free, and tipping the driver through Grazzy is optional.

1Motivation

Who has the problem. Many Yale SOM students, especially Executive MBA students in for class weekends, stay at Hotel Marcel and use its complimentary car service to get to and from Evans Hall. We all leave around the same time every morning and come back around the same time every evening.

How it works today. In practice the hotel's app (ride.hotelmarcel.com) is a one-way request. You send it in and then hear nothing back:

  • no live view of where the driver is or how far along the trip is;
  • no ETA;
  • no way to message the driver;
  • no booking in advance (the request page says to call the front desk for later rides);
  • requests only during staffed hours, so nothing off-hours.

What it costs. Students stand in the lobby not knowing if a car is two minutes or twenty minutes away, and some give up and pay for an Uber. Everyone requests at once at 7:45 AM, so the queue piles up right before class. Front-desk staff field "where's my car?" calls and phone bookings. Drivers can't say "I'm at the side entrance."

Measured cost (wait times, Uber spend, desk calls) goes here once the rider survey is done (see Impact).

The twist planned. On its own, "Uber for a hotel" is a version of something that already exists. What's new is the commute pattern: dozens of students at one hotel, one destination, the same few departure windows. Instead of N separate requests, students join shared runs (for example, the 7:40 AM and 8:00 AM runs to SOM, and 5:30 PM back), see how many seats are left, and get one live-tracked car. An AI coordinator handles plain-language bookings ("get me to SOM by 8:15 every class day this weekend") and fills runs so the hotel moves more students with the same cars.

4Tokenomics estimate only

Assumptions: about 3,000 input and 300 output tokens per booking chat, at luna's course prices of $0.10 per million input and $0.50 per million output tokens. Each student books about 2 rides a day, 22 days a month.

These are estimates. The rubric needs measured input, cached and output tokens from real runs, and gives half credit for estimates. Cost-cutting plan: cache the fixed run schedule and system prompt, and handle "book my usual" with no model call.

The first three are checked by the repo's automated test (node tests/e2e.mjs, 18 checks passing). These defenses protect the app; the final project also needs a defense shown stopping an attack on the agent.

6Impact to measure

Plan: a 2-minute survey of SOM students who stay at Marcel, before and after trying the app. It asks about lobby wait time, how often they gave up and paid for an Uber (and how much), and whether they'd book ahead. One or two classmates also try the live app on their phones during a class weekend.

7Limits and next steps

  • Demo only: the map is a drawing and the car is simulated; everything resets on refresh. Next, a small server that saves rides and chat and sends live updates, plus real GPS from the driver's phone.
  • No AI yet: next, build the booking and dispatch agents above on gpt-6-luna and measure their real token use.
  • Shared runs: seats per run, a "join the 8:00 AM run" button, and filling runs from class schedules.
  • Off-hours: let students book ahead overnight so the desk can confirm in the morning.
  • Hotel adoption: a proposal for Hotel Marcel covering a pilot, privacy controls and how it fits their existing app.

An independent student concept, not affiliated with or endorsed by Hotel Marcel or Grazzy. Built from public pages only, with no guest data.
